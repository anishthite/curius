What is this?

This is a public, read-only map of Curius: who follows whom, what pages people save, and which sources show up often enough to shape the shared reading room.

The graph starts with a simple directed edge: one person follows another. From thousands of those edges, the page can show dense neighborhoods, peripheral islands, bridge people, and likely paths between readers. The companion front page looks at the saved links and highlights that travel through the same network.

It is not a ranking of people or a claim about influence. It is a way to inspect a public social archive: where attention pools, which domains become common ground, and where the follow graph suggests adjacent readers.

How do interests travel?

Among 19,244 follow edges where both people have saved-link domains, 86.8% share at least one domain.

The overlap is broad rather than precise: large public domains become shared channels, while smaller blogs and papers make the graph interesting when you zoom into neighborhoods.
